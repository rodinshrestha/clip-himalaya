# Content Guidelines — Clip Himalaya

These rules apply to all customer-facing copy: trek descriptions, region pages,
FAQs, blog posts, and marketing text.

---

## Voice

Write like a seasoned guide who has actually walked these trails — not a travel
brochure writer. Warm, knowledgeable, grounded. You've seen the sunrise from
Kala Patthar, you know which teahouse in Dingboche has the best dal bhat, and
you're honest about when a trek is going to be hard.

## Tone principles

- **Concrete over abstract.** Name the village, the elevation, the river. "You
  cross a suspension bridge over the Dudh Kosi at 2,600m" beats "you cross
  stunning bridges."
- **Honest over promotional.** If day 7 is a grind, say so. If the teahouse
  mattress is thin, say that too. Credibility earns trust faster than polish.
- **Active voice.** "You climb 800m to Namche" not "800m of elevation is gained."
- **Short paragraphs.** Three to four sentences max. Readers are scanning on
  phones between packing their bags.
- **Scannable structure.** Use headings, bullet points, and quick-facts bars.
  Nobody reads a wall of text about permits.

## Words and phrases to avoid

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| journey of a lifetime | (just describe the trek) |
| breathtaking vistas | name what you actually see |
| majestic peaks | Annapurna South, Machapuchare, Ama Dablam |
| awe-inspiring | (cut it — show, don't tell) |
| trip of a lifetime | (same as above) |
| nestled | located, sits at, built into |
| embark on | start, begin, head out |
| adventurers | trekkers, hikers |
| stunning | (be specific about what makes it striking) |
| pristine wilderness | name the place |
| once-in-a-lifetime | (drop it) |

## Structure for trek pages

1. **Hero hook** — one honest sentence below the title. Not a tagline. What
   makes this specific trek worth doing.
2. **Quick facts bar** — duration, max elevation, difficulty, best seasons,
   start/end points, permits. Scannable at a glance.
3. **Description** — 2-3 paragraphs. What the trail is actually like. Name
   villages, rivers, landmarks. What changes as you gain altitude.
4. **Day-by-day itinerary** — each day gets: title, elevation change, walking
   hours, and a short description of what the day looks like. Be specific.
5. **Included / not included** — plain lists. No marketing fluff.
6. **Fitness guidance** — be honest. What fitness level is actually needed.
   What training helps. Where people struggle.
7. **Accommodation & food** — what teahouses are like at different altitudes.
   Charging, WiFi, hot showers. What the food is. Where the best dal bhat is.
8. **Permits** — which ones, approximate cost, who arranges them.
9. **Packing essentials** — short list, 8-12 items. Not a 60-item checklist.
10. **FAQ** — 3-5 questions specific to this trek. Altitude, connectivity,
    rest days, booking timing.
11. **CTA** — soft. "Tell us your dates and group size." Plus a "customize
    this trek" option. Never hard-sell.

## Structure for region pages

1. **Hero** — region name, one-sentence tagline, elevation range.
2. **Character** — what makes this region distinct from the other two.
   Geography, landscape changes, what you see.
3. **Info cards** — Culture & People, Best Seasons, Who It Suits.
4. **Trek listing** — clean grid of available treks with cards.

## Numbers and facts

- Always verify elevation figures, permit costs, and walking times before
  publishing. These change and outdated numbers erode trust.
- Use meters for elevation (with commas: 5,364m not 5364m).
- Use "hours" not "hrs" in running text. Abbreviate in quick-facts bars.
- Express difficulty as: Easy, Moderate, Moderate-Hard, Hard, Strenuous.
- Date ranges as: March-May, September-November (no spaces around dash).

## Images

- Every trek page needs at minimum: 1 hero, 3-5 trail/landscape shots,
  1 culture/village shot, 1 teahouse/accommodation shot.
- Alt text must be descriptive and contextual, never generic ("image", "banner").
  Good: "Prayer flags on the trail to Everest Base Camp at 4,900m".
- All sourced images must be documented in `IMAGE_CREDITS.md` with license
  and source ID.

## Updating content

All trek and region content lives in Sanity Studio (`/admin`). Changes take
effect within 30 seconds (ISR revalidation). No code deploy needed for
content edits.

SEO fields (`seoTitle`, `seoDescription`, `ogImage`) are available on trek
and activity documents under the collapsible "SEO & Social Sharing" fieldset.
Use these to override auto-generated meta tags when needed.
